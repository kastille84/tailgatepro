# TBT-220 Mobile Device Security

## Why It Matters
Mobile Device Security matters because a data or security breach can cause serious harm to customers and the business, and often starts with a simple mistake.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Loss or theft of devices containing company/customer data
- phishing and social engineering
- weak or shared passwords
- improper disposal of confidential documents

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Keep devices locked and password-protected
- verify unexpected requests for information or payment before acting
- use strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication where available
- dispose of confidential documents securely (shredding)

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Lock devices when not in use
- report suspicious emails or requests before acting on them
- keep company and customer information confidential
- report lost or stolen devices immediately

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Share passwords or login details
- leave devices or documents unattended in vehicles or public places
- click links or attachments from unknown or suspicious senders

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Update software and passwords regularly
- double check the sender's email address on unexpected requests
- keep personal and work accounts separate

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Report a suspected breach or lost device immediately
- change passwords if a compromise is suspected
- do not attempt to hide or delay reporting a mistake

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Mobile Device Security on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
