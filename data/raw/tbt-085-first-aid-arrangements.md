# TBT-085 First Aid Arrangements

## Why It Matters
First Aid Arrangements matters because accurate and prompt reporting is what allows hazards to be fixed before they cause a more serious incident.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Unreported near misses masking developing hazards
- delayed reporting losing important evidence
- incomplete investigation
- repeat incidents from unaddressed root causes

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Report all incidents and near misses promptly, however minor
- preserve the scene where practicable
- gather witness details at the time
- cooperate fully with any investigation

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Report every accident, incident and near miss, no matter how small
- preserve the scene until told otherwise
- provide accurate information to the investigation
- seek first aid/medical attention for any injury

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Delay reporting an incident
- disturb the scene of a serious incident before it has been recorded
- assume someone else has reported it

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Report near misses even when nothing was damaged or no one was hurt
- note down what you saw as soon as possible while it's fresh
- support colleagues in reporting without fear of blame

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Ensure any injured person receives first aid/medical attention first
- notify your supervisor immediately
- follow the RIDDOR process where the incident meets the reporting criteria

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of First Aid Arrangements on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
