# TBT-294 Spare Parts Safety

## Why It Matters
Spare Parts Safety matters because planned and well-controlled maintenance prevents the failures that lead to breakdowns, downtime and safety incidents.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Deferred maintenance leading to unexpected failure
- incorrect or substandard spare parts
- inadequate isolation during maintenance
- poor handover between maintenance visits

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Follow the planned maintenance schedule
- use correct, approved spare parts
- isolate and lock off before maintenance work
- record findings and actions clearly for the next visit

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Follow the maintenance schedule and checklist for the equipment
- use approved spare parts and torque/settings from the manufacturer
- isolate before carrying out maintenance
- leave clear, accurate records for the next visit

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Skip scheduled maintenance checks to save time
- fit non-approved or unknown-origin spare parts to safety-critical equipment
- leave defects unrecorded or unescalated

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Flag developing issues before they become failures
- keep spare parts traceable to their source
- photograph before/after condition for reference

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Isolate and make safe immediately if a fault is found during maintenance
- escalate defects that affect safety before leaving site
- do not return equipment to service until it is safe to do so

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Spare Parts Safety on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
