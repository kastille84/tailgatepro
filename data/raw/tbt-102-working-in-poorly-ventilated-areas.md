# TBT-102 Working in Poorly Ventilated Areas

## Why It Matters
Working in Poorly Ventilated Areas matters because confined spaces can contain hazards that are invisible until they cause serious harm — atmosphere testing and rescue arrangements are not optional.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Oxygen deficiency or enrichment
- toxic or flammable gas build-up
- engulfment
- restricted means of escape
- difficulty raising the alarm

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Avoid entry to a confined space wherever possible
- obtain a confined space entry permit
- test the atmosphere before and during entry
- have a trained standby person and rescue arrangements in place before entry

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Hold a valid confined space permit before entry
- test the atmosphere before entering and continuously monitor during the work
- have a standby person and communication method at all times
- know the rescue arrangements before you enter

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Enter a confined space without a permit and atmosphere test
- enter alone without a standby person
- attempt a rescue yourself without the correct training and equipment

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep entry/exit times logged
- brief the standby person on what to do if contact is lost
- ventilate the space before and during work where practicable

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Raise the alarm immediately — do not enter to rescue without trained rescue personnel and equipment
- call the emergency services
- keep the standby person at the entry point

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Working in Poorly Ventilated Areas on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
