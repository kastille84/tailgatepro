# TBT-094 Dynamic Risk Assessment

## Why It Matters
Dynamic Risk Assessment matters because a risk assessment or method statement that isn't read, understood and followed on the day offers no protection at all.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Task risks not covered by the existing RAMS
- changed site conditions since the assessment was written
- team members unfamiliar with the document
- generic assessments not matched to the actual task

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Read and sign the RAMS before starting work
- stop and reassess if conditions differ from what's described
- raise anything missing or unclear with your supervisor
- keep the RAMS accessible on site during the work

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Read and understand the RAMS/risk assessment before starting
- sign to confirm you have read and understood it
- stop work and seek guidance if the task doesn't match the assessment
- follow the control measures specified

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Start a task without reading the relevant RAMS
- carry out work that isn't covered by an assessment without getting one written
- ignore control measures because they seem inconvenient

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Discuss the RAMS as a team before starting, not just sign it
- flag site conditions that differ from the assessment
- suggest improvements based on what you see on the ground

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop work immediately if conditions are more hazardous than assessed
- seek a dynamic risk assessment from your supervisor
- do not resume until controls are updated

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Dynamic Risk Assessment on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
