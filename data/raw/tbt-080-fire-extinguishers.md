# TBT-080 Fire Extinguishers

## Why It Matters
Fire Extinguishers matters because fire can develop and spread rapidly, and everyone on site needs to know how to prevent, detect and respond to it.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Ignition sources near combustible materials
- blocked escape routes
- inadequate/incorrect fire extinguishers
- delayed evacuation

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Control ignition sources, especially during hot work
- keep escape routes and fire points clear
- know the location and type of extinguishers for the area
- follow the site fire and evacuation procedure

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Know the evacuation procedure and assembly point for every site you work on
- report blocked escape routes or missing/discharged extinguishers
- control hot work with a permit and fire watch
- raise the alarm immediately if you discover a fire

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Prop open fire doors
- obstruct extinguishers, alarm points or escape routes
- attempt to fight a fire beyond your training or the capability of the extinguisher

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Locate the nearest extinguisher and exit when you arrive on a new site
- keep flammable materials away from ignition sources
- report faulty fire equipment

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Raise the alarm and evacuate immediately
- go to the designated assembly point
- call the fire service
- do not re-enter the building until told it is safe

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Fire Extinguishers on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
