# TBT-027 Pressure Testing

## Why It Matters
Pressure Testing matters because stored pressure that is released uncontrolled can cause serious injury from projectiles, jets or sudden component failure.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Sudden release of stored pressure
- component failure/projectiles
- high-pressure jets
- incorrect test media or over-pressurisation

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Depressurise and isolate systems before opening them up
- use gauges rated for the expected pressure
- carry out pressure tests to the written procedure with exclusion zones in place
- never exceed the design/test pressure of the system

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Isolate and depressurise before breaking into pipework
- use correctly rated and calibrated gauges
- keep people clear of the test area during pressure testing
- follow the written test procedure and record results

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Work on pressurised systems without isolating them first
- exceed the rated test pressure
- stand in line with pipework, valves or joints under test
- use damaged or uncalibrated gauges

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Warn others before pressure testing begins
- barrier off the test area
- check joints methodically and record readings

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Isolate the pressure source immediately if a failure occurs
- clear the area
- treat any injury and report the incident
- do not attempt to repair under pressure

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Pressure Testing on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
