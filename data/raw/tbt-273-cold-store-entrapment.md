# TBT-273 Cold Store Entrapment

## Why It Matters
Cold Store Entrapment matters because cold rooms and freezers present entrapment, cold-injury and asphyxiation risks that aren't always obvious from outside.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Entrapment inside a cold room/freezer
- cold stress/frostbite
- oxygen displacement from refrigerant leaks
- slippery surfaces from ice/condensation

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Check the internal safety release/escape mechanism before entering
- never disable the internal door release
- limit time inside and wear appropriate cold-weather PPE
- ventilate and test the atmosphere if a refrigerant leak is suspected

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check the internal escape release works before entering a cold room
- let someone know when entering and expected duration
- wear appropriate PPE for the temperature
- report any fault with door releases or alarms immediately

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Enter a cold room without checking the escape release
- disable or wedge open the internal safety release
- enter alone if there's any doubt about the escape mechanism or ventilation

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep cold room floors free of ice build-up
- test panic/escape alarms periodically
- keep a torch or means of communication when entering large cold stores

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Use the internal escape release or alarm immediately if trapped
- raise the alarm if a colleague is believed trapped and do not delay
- ventilate before re-entry if a refrigerant leak is suspected

- What are the main hazards of Cold Store Entrapment on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
