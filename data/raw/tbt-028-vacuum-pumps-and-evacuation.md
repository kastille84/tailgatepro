# TBT-028 Vacuum Pumps and Evacuation

## Why It Matters
Vacuum Pumps and Evacuation matters because an improperly evacuated system risks moisture and non-condensables remaining in the circuit, and vacuum pump handling has its own hazards.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Oil mist/fumes from the pump exhaust
- manual handling of the pump
- electrical hazards from pump motors
- residual pressure in the system before connecting

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Confirm the system is fully depressurised before connecting the vacuum pump
- run the pump in a ventilated area
- check oil level and condition before use
- use a calibrated micron gauge to confirm the target vacuum

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Confirm zero pressure before connecting evacuation equipment
- position the pump so exhaust does not affect anyone nearby
- record the vacuum level achieved and hold time
- isolate the pump electrically before servicing it

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Connect a vacuum pump to a pressurised system
- leave a running pump unattended in an unventilated space
- use pump oil past its service life without checking condition

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Change pump oil regularly per manufacturer guidance
- use core removal tools to speed evacuation
- keep hoses in good condition to avoid false readings

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Switch off and isolate the pump if a fault develops
- ventilate the area if fumes build up
- report any electrical fault immediately
