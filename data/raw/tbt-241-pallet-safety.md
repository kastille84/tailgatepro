# TBT-241 Pallet Safety

## Why It Matters
Pallet Safety matters because poor storage and racking practices can lead to falling loads, collapses and access route hazards.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Falling stock from overloaded or damaged racking
- collision with stored materials
- blocked access/egress routes
- unstable stacking

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Check racking load limits and inspection status before loading
- stack materials safely with heavier items lower down
- keep aisles and access routes clear
- report damaged racking immediately and take it out of use

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check the safe working load before loading racking
- stack and store materials so they cannot fall or topple
- keep gangways and fire routes clear
- report racking damage immediately

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Overload racking or shelving beyond its rated capacity
- climb racking to access stock
- store items in a way that blocks access, escape routes or fire equipment

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Label racking with load limits clearly
- carry out regular racking inspections
- store heavier or bulkier items at lower levels

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Cordon off collapsed or damaged racking immediately
- report the incident and do not attempt to re-stack until inspected
- seek first aid for anyone struck by falling materials

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Pallet Safety on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
