# TBT-002 Manual Handling

## Why It Matters
Manual Handling matters because poor manual handling technique is one of the leading causes of lost-time injury in our industry.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Heavy or awkward loads (compressors, plant, cylinders)
- repetitive lifting
- twisting while carrying
- restricted access routes
- sharp edges on components

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Assess the load before lifting (TILE: Task, Individual, Load, Environment)
- use mechanical aids or team lifts for heavy/awkward items
- plan the route and clear obstructions first
- break large loads into smaller ones where possible

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Assess every load before lifting it
- use the correct lifting technique
- ask for help or mechanical assistance with heavy or awkward loads
- report back pain or strain immediately

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Lift beyond your safe capability
- twist while carrying a load
- attempt to lift alone when the task calls for two people or an aid

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep loads close to the body
- plan the route before you lift
- use trolleys, hoists or gin wheels where available

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop lifting immediately if you feel strain or pain
- put the load down safely
- seek first aid
- report the incident

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Manual Handling on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
