# TBT-179 Spill Kits

## Why It Matters
Spill Kits matters because environmental incidents can cause lasting harm and carry legal duties that apply to everyone on site, not just management.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Pollution of land, water or drains
- uncontrolled refrigerant or oil release
- incorrect waste segregation
- excessive noise or dust affecting neighbours

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Use drip trays and spill kits when handling oils/chemicals
- segregate waste correctly at source
- protect drains before work that could cause a spill
- report environmental incidents immediately, however minor

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Use spill kits and drain protection where there's a risk of a release
- segregate waste into the correct containers
- recover refrigerant fully rather than venting it
- report any environmental incident promptly

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Allow oil, refrigerant, chemicals or wash water to enter drains or watercourses
- dispose of hazardous waste in general waste
- burn or bury waste on site

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep spill kits accessible wherever oils or chemicals are used
- check waste is going to the correct, licensed carrier
- minimise noise and disruption to neighbours where practicable

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Contain a spill immediately using the nearest spill kit
- prevent the substance reaching a drain or watercourse
- report the incident to your supervisor and, where required, the Environment Agency/NIEA

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Spill Kits on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
