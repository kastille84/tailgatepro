# TBT-053 Roof Access

## Why It Matters
Roof Access matters because a fall from height remains one of the most common causes of serious injury and fatality in construction and building services.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Falls from height
- falling objects striking people below
- unstable or incorrectly erected access equipment
- overreaching
- adverse weather affecting access equipment

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Use the safest practicable means of access (avoid height where possible)
- inspect access equipment before use and check the inspection tag
- maintain 3 points of contact
- use edge protection or fall arrest where a fall risk remains
- exclude people from below the work area

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Use only inspected, tagged access equipment
- wear fall protection where required and clip on at all times when using it
- keep the work area below barriered off
- stop work in high winds or poor weather

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Overreach or lean out from access equipment
- use damaged, modified or unsuitable access equipment
- work at height alone without the required controls in place
- carry tools or materials in a way that risks a fall

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Plan tasks to minimise time spent at height
- keep tools tethered
- check the ground/surface below before erecting equipment

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Raise the alarm immediately for any fall
- do not move a casualty unless in immediate danger
- call the emergency services
- preserve the scene for investigation

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Roof Access on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
