# TBT-248 Compressed Gas Storage

## Why It Matters
Compressed Gas Storage matters because gas cylinders under pressure can become dangerous projectiles or ignition sources if damaged or handled incorrectly.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Cylinder falling or being struck
- uncontrolled gas release
- fire/explosion
- asphyxiation in confined spaces
- cold burns from escaping gas

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Secure cylinders upright with a chain or strap at all times
- use trolleys to move cylinders, never roll or drag them
- keep valves protected with caps when not in use
- store oxidising and fuel gases separately and away from heat

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Secure and cap cylinders when not connected
- check hoses, regulators and connections before use
- transport cylinders upright and restrained
- close valves and bleed lines after use

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Drop, roll or drag cylinders
- use damaged or unlabelled cylinders
- store fuel and oxidising gases together
- expose cylinders to heat sources or direct sun for long periods

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Check cylinder contents and pressure before starting a job
- keep valve keys with the cylinder in use
- return empties promptly and mark them as empty

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Evacuate and isolate the area for a leaking cylinder
- shut off the valve if it is safe to do so
- do not use naked flame or create sparks nearby
- call the emergency services for uncontrolled releases
