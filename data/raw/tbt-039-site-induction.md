# TBT-039 Site Induction

## Why It Matters
Site Induction matters because working on customer premises brings additional risks from unfamiliar hazards, other occupiers and equipment we don't control.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Unfamiliar site hazards
- interaction with customer staff/public
- conflicting site rules
- damage to customer property
- working around live production/food areas

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Complete site induction and sign in/out
- agree isolation and access arrangements with the customer before starting
- protect customer property and work areas
- follow site-specific rules in addition to our own

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Sign in and complete any required site induction
- confirm isolations and permits with the client's responsible person
- keep the work area tidy and protect customer property
- report incidents to both your employer and the client as required

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Start work without the client's agreement/permit where required
- use customer equipment without authorisation
- leave the work area or plant in an unsafe condition

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Introduce yourself to the client contact on arrival
- photograph the work area before and after
- leave the site as clean as you found it

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Follow the client's site emergency procedure
- notify the client's responsible person and your supervisor
- report the incident through our own system as well

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Site Induction on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
