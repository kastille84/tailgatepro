# TBT-141 Occupational Health

## Why It Matters
Occupational Health matters because extremes of temperature and personal wellbeing directly affect a person's ability to work safely.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Heat stress/heat exhaustion
- cold stress in plant rooms and cold stores
- dehydration
- fatigue affecting concentration and decision-making

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Take regular breaks and stay hydrated in hot conditions
- wear appropriate insulated clothing for cold environments
- monitor yourself and colleagues for signs of heat or cold stress
- plan demanding tasks around the coolest/warmest parts of the day where possible

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Stay hydrated and take breaks in hot conditions
- wear the cold-weather PPE provided for cold store/plant room work
- recognise and report symptoms of heat or cold stress in yourself or others
- get adequate rest before working days

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Ignore symptoms of heat exhaustion, cold stress or fatigue
- work in extreme conditions without the appropriate breaks and PPE
- continue a task if you feel unwell

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Check on colleagues working in extreme environments
- keep water available on hot days
- limit continuous time in cold stores/freezers

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Move the affected person to a suitable environment immediately
- seek first aid/medical attention for heat or cold stress symptoms
- report the incident

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Occupational Health on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
