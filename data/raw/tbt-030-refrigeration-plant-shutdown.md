# TBT-030 Refrigeration Plant Shutdown

## Why It Matters
Refrigeration Plant Shutdown matters because incorrect start-up or shutdown sequencing can damage plant, release refrigerant or expose people to moving/electrical hazards.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Unexpected start-up of rotating equipment
- pressure surges
- electrical hazards during start-up checks
- release of refrigerant or oil during shutdown

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Follow the manufacturer's start-up/shutdown sequence
- check guards and isolators are correctly positioned before starting
- warn others in the area before starting plant
- monitor pressures and temperatures during start-up

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Follow the written start-up/shutdown procedure for the plant
- check the area is clear before starting rotating equipment
- monitor gauges during start-up and shut down immediately if readings are abnormal
- leave the plant in a safe, labelled state after shutdown

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Bypass the standard start-up sequence to save time
- leave plant running unattended during an abnormal start-up
- restart plant that has tripped without establishing the cause

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Log start-up and shutdown readings
- brief colleagues before starting large plant
- check isolators are in the correct position before beginning

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Use the emergency stop if plant behaves abnormally
- isolate the plant
- report the fault before any attempt to restart
