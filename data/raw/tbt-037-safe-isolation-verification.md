# TBT-037 Safe Isolation Verification

## Why It Matters
Safe Isolation Verification matters because failure to properly isolate and verify equipment before work begins is one of the most common causes of serious electrical and mechanical incidents.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Unexpected re-energisation
- residual/stored energy
- incorrect isolation point
- multiple people working without coordinated lock-off

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Identify all energy sources (electrical, mechanical, pressure, gravity)
- isolate and lock off with your own padlock and tag
- prove the isolation is effective before starting work
- use a multi-lock hasp where more than one person is working

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Lock off with your own padlock before starting work
- prove isolation before touching the equipment
- remove your lock only when your work is complete and it is safe to do so
- keep isolation certificates with the job

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Rely on someone else's isolation without applying your own lock
- remove another person's lock
- assume equipment is isolated without testing/proving it

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Tag isolators with your name and the date
- communicate isolation status to the whole team
- check stored energy (springs, capacitors, gravity loads) has also been controlled

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- If equipment energises unexpectedly, stop work and isolate again immediately
- report the failure
- do not resume until the cause is identified and corrected

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Safe Isolation Verification on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
