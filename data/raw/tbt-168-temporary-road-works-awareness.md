# TBT-168 Temporary Road Works Awareness

## Why It Matters
Temporary Road Works Awareness matters because members of the public and building occupants may be unaware of our work and its hazards, so we carry the responsibility for keeping them safe.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Public or occupants entering the work area unaware of hazards
- trip/impact hazards from our equipment in shared areas
- unauthorised access to site or vehicles
- children or vulnerable persons near the work area

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Barrier and sign the work area clearly
- keep tools, materials and equipment secured when unattended
- never leave access points unsecured
- be alert to children or vulnerable persons in the vicinity

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Barrier off and sign the work area before starting
- secure tools and materials so they cannot be accessed by others
- challenge or report unauthorised persons in the work area
- keep vehicles locked when unattended

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Leave the work area unsecured, even for a short break
- allow untrained persons into the work area
- leave hazardous materials or tools accessible to the public

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Introduce yourself to occupants working nearby
- use physical barriers, not just signage, where practical
- plan deliveries/access to minimise disruption to occupants

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Make the area safe immediately if a member of the public is at risk
- call for first aid/emergency services if needed
- report the incident, including near misses involving the public

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Temporary Road Works Awareness on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
