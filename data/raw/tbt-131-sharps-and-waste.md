# TBT-131 Sharps and Waste

## Why It Matters
Sharps and Waste matters because biological hazards are often invisible and can cause serious illness from what looks like a routine task.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Legionella exposure from water systems
- contact with contaminated water or waste
- sharps injuries
- airborne biological contamination (e.g. asbestos, mould)

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Follow the site's legionella/water treatment procedure before disturbing water systems
- assume materials may contain asbestos in pre-2000 buildings until surveyed
- use sharps containers and never re-sheath needles by hand
- wear appropriate PPE when handling contaminated materials

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check for an asbestos survey before disturbing suspect materials
- follow legionella control procedures when working on water systems
- use sharps bins for any sharps encountered
- report suspected biological hazards before continuing work

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Disturb suspected asbestos-containing materials
- work on water systems without following the legionella procedure
- dispose of sharps or biological waste in general waste

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Wash hands thoroughly after work involving water systems or waste
- keep cuts and grazes covered
- report standing water or poor water system maintenance

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop work immediately if asbestos is suspected and evacuate the area
- seek medical attention for sharps injuries and report immediately
- isolate the area and contact your supervisor

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Sharps and Waste on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
