# Ammonia Refrigeration - Hazard Recognition

Anhydrous ammonia is used as a refrigerant in meat and poultry processing, dairy, breweries, juice plants, cold storage warehouses, and petrochemical operations.

"Ammonia spills and releases pose a significant threat to workers from skin contact, inhalation, and fire and explosion."

Process hazard analysis (PHA) systematically evaluates what could fail and what safeguards are needed against releases.

Referenced resources: EPA Accident Prevention Manual; NIOSH toxicology data on acute exposure and safe concentrations; ATSDR health profiles; IIAR technical standards.

Case studies: hydraulic shock failures causing ammonia releases; galvanic corrosion causing catastrophic pipe failure near brine tanks.

Related OSHA ammonia eTool excerpts (osha.gov/etools/ammonia-refrigeration): ammonia is corrosive to skin, eyes and lungs; 300 ppm is IDLH; flammable at about 15-28% by volume in air; flush eyes/skin with water for 15+ minutes; use ammonia-impervious gloves (neoprene), boots, and protective clothing during spills.
