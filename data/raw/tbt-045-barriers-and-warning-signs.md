# TBT-045 Barriers and Warning Signs

## Why It Matters
Barriers and Warning Signs matters because poor housekeeping is behind a large proportion of slip, trip and fall injuries, and undermines every other control on site.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Slips on spilt liquids/refrigerant oil
- trips over cables, hoses and materials
- falling materials from poor stacking
- obstructed escape routes

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Keep work areas, walkways and escape routes clear
- clean up spills immediately and use appropriate absorbents
- store materials and tools tidily, not left on the floor
- route cables and hoses to avoid trip hazards

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Keep your work area tidy throughout the job, not just at the end
- clean up spills as they happen
- store tools and materials safely when not in use
- report blocked escape routes or fire points

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Leave tools, cables or materials as trip hazards
- block fire exits, extinguishers or escape routes
- leave spills unattended or unmarked

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Do a 5-minute tidy-up before breaks and at the end of the shift
- use cable covers/ramps where cables must cross walkways
- bag and remove waste as you generate it

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Cordon off and clean any spill immediately
- report slips/trips even if no one was hurt
- check escape routes remain clear at all times

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Barriers and Warning Signs on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
