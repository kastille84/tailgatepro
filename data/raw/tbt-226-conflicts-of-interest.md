# TBT-226 Conflicts of Interest

## Why It Matters
Conflicts of Interest matters because how we buy, select suppliers and conduct business reflects directly on the company's integrity and legal compliance.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Use of unapproved or counterfeit parts/materials
- conflicts of interest influencing decisions
- bribery or improper gifts
- supply chain practices that breach modern slavery requirements

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Use only approved suppliers and genuine parts
- declare any conflict of interest before it influences a decision
- follow the company gifts and hospitality policy
- raise concerns about supplier practices through the correct channel

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Source parts and materials from approved suppliers
- declare gifts, hospitality or conflicts of interest as required by policy
- report suspected bribery, fraud or unethical practice
- check safety data/certification on purchased items before use

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Use unapproved or unverified suppliers for safety-critical parts
- accept gifts or hospitality that could influence a business decision
- turn a blind eye to suspected unethical practice in the supply chain

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep records of supplier checks and approvals
- ask if unsure whether a gift or arrangement is acceptable
- report anything that doesn't look right, even if you're not certain

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Report suspected fraud, bribery or serious ethical breaches promptly and confidentially
- follow the whistleblowing procedure if unsure how to raise a concern

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Conflicts of Interest on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
