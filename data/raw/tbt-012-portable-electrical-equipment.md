# TBT-012 Portable Electrical Equipment

## Why It Matters
Portable Electrical Equipment matters because unsafe electrical work or damaged equipment can cause fatal shock, burns or fire.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Electric shock
- arc flash/burns
- unexpected energisation
- fire from faulty equipment
- damage to cables/equipment

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Only competent, authorised persons carry out electrical work
- isolate, lock off and prove dead before starting work
- use a suitable voltage indicator and proving unit
- inspect equipment and cables before use

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Isolate and prove dead before working on electrical equipment
- use lock-off devices and your own padlock
- test the isolation with an approved voltage indicator
- report defects and remove damaged equipment from use

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Work on live equipment unless a specific live-working procedure and permit is in place
- assume equipment is isolated without proving it dead
- bypass or remove electrical protective devices
- use damaged leads, plugs or equipment

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep isolation certificates/permits with the equipment while work is in progress
- label isolated circuits clearly
- carry a spare proving unit battery

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Do not touch a casualty in contact with live equipment — isolate the supply first
- call emergency services
- use a CPR-trained first aider if breathing has stopped
- report the incident immediately
