# TBT-298 Tagging Out Defective Equipment

## Why It Matters
Tagging Out Defective Equipment matters because poorly maintained or uncontrolled tools and equipment are a common source of both injury and dropped-object hazards.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Use of defective or uncalibrated tools/equipment
- dropped tools, especially at height
- loss of tool control leading to unaccounted equipment on site
- damage from using the wrong tool for the task

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Carry out and record pre-use checks on tools and equipment
- tether tools when working at height
- take defective equipment out of service and tag it clearly
- keep a tool inventory/control system up to date

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check tools and equipment before use
- tether tools when working at height
- tag and remove defective equipment from service immediately
- return tools to storage/control at the end of the task

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Use equipment that has failed a pre-use check
- leave tools unsecured at height
- return a defective tool to general use without it being repaired/inspected

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep a simple log of tool condition and last inspection date
- store tools in a way that protects them from damage
- report tools that go missing so they can be accounted for

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop using equipment immediately if a defect is found
- report dropped objects even if no one was hurt
- tag and quarantine defective equipment

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Tagging Out Defective Equipment on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
