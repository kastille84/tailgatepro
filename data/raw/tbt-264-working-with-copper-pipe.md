# TBT-264 Working with Copper Pipe

## Why It Matters
Working with Copper Pipe matters because pipework can be pressurised, hot, cold or carry refrigerant, and incorrect handling risks both injury and system failure.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Contact with hot or extremely cold pipe surfaces
- sharp edges from cut pipe
- pressurised residue in pipework
- brazing/soldering fire risk near pipework

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Confirm the system is isolated and depressurised before cutting into pipework
- support pipework adequately during installation
- deburr cut ends and protect against sharp edges
- use appropriate PPE for hot/cold surface contact

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Confirm isolation and depressurisation before working on pipework
- support and fix pipework to the specification
- deburr and clean cut ends before joining
- protect nearby materials from heat when brazing

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Cut into pipework that hasn't been confirmed isolated and depressurised
- leave pipework unsupported creating strain on joints
- leave sharp or burred pipe ends exposed

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Check pipe supports at the correct spacing per specification
- pressure test joints before insulating/closing in
- keep offcuts tidy and dispose of them correctly

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Isolate immediately if pipework fails or leaks
- evacuate if refrigerant or gas is released
- report the incident and do not attempt a temporary repair under pressure
