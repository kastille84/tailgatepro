# TBT-011 Power Tools

## Why It Matters
Power Tools matters because incorrect selection or use of tools is a frequent cause of cuts, entanglement and eye injuries.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Contact with moving parts or cutting edges
- flying debris/dust
- entanglement
- electric shock from damaged equipment
- vibration exposure

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Select the right tool for the job
- carry out a pre-use visual check (cable, guard, condition)
- use guards, dust extraction and eye protection
- keep cutting edges sharp and maintained
- isolate power before changing accessories

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Inspect tools before use
- use the guard and PPE provided
- isolate/unplug before changing blades, bits or accessories
- report damaged tools and take them out of service

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Remove or bypass guards
- use a tool for a task it was not designed for
- use a tool with a damaged cable, casing or guard
- carry a running tool by its cable/hose

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Keep the work area tidy to avoid trip hazards from cables/hoses
- return tools to storage after use
- use low-vibration tools where available for prolonged tasks

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Isolate power immediately
- apply first aid for cuts or injuries
- report the incident and retain the tool for inspection

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Power Tools on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
