# TBT-062 Manual Hoists

## Why It Matters
Manual Hoists matters because lifting operations involve suspended loads that can cause fatal injury if equipment fails or the load is not controlled.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Load falling or swinging
- equipment failure (slings, hoists, accessories)
- people working or standing under a suspended load
- incorrect rated capacity for the load

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Only use lifting equipment that is in date for thorough examination
- plan the lift and confirm the load weight and rated capacity
- exclude people from under and around the load
- use a trained banksman/slinger for the operation

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check the lifting equipment's inspection tag/certificate before use
- confirm the safe working load before lifting
- keep clear of suspended loads
- follow the lift plan for anything non-routine

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Use lifting equipment that is out of test/inspection
- exceed the rated capacity of any accessory
- stand or work under a suspended load
- improvise lifting points not designed for lifting

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Tag and store lifting accessories correctly when not in use
- double-check sling angles and load weight before lifting
- communicate clearly with hand signals or radio during the lift

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Lower the load to a safe position if a fault develops, if it is safe to do so
- clear the area
- report equipment failures immediately and take the item out of service

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Manual Hoists on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
