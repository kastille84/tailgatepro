# TBT-173 Refrigerant Environmental Controls

## Why It Matters
Refrigerant Environmental Controls matters because refrigerant is a pressurised, potentially harmful substance and mishandling risks both personal injury and environmental release.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Pressure release/cold burns
- asphyxiation in confined or poorly ventilated spaces
- chemical exposure
- fire risk with flammable (A2L/A3) refrigerants
- environmental release and reporting obligations

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Use only F-Gas certified, competent personnel
- recover refrigerant into approved cylinders rather than venting
- ventilate the work area and use a refrigerant detector where required
- follow the site/company refrigerant handling procedure

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Hold the relevant F-Gas qualification for the work being carried out
- recover refrigerant fully before opening the system
- use approved recovery cylinders and equipment
- record refrigerant quantities recovered/charged

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Vent refrigerant to atmosphere
- mix refrigerant types
- overfill recovery cylinders beyond their rated capacity
- work on refrigerant systems without the correct PPE (gloves, eye protection)

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Leak-check connections before leaving site
- label cylinders with refrigerant type and weight
- keep the refrigerant log up to date

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Evacuate and ventilate the area for a significant leak
- avoid ignition sources near flammable refrigerants
- use RPE if entry is unavoidable and trained/equipped to do so
- report any release as required by the F-Gas regulations
