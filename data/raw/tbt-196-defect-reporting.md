# TBT-196 Defect Reporting

## Why It Matters
Defect Reporting matters because getting it right first time protects the customer, the company's reputation and often safety itself — rework and shortcuts create new risks.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Non-conforming work being handed over undetected
- incorrect materials or specifications used
- inadequate records for warranty/compliance purposes
- rework introducing new hazards

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Work to the current drawing/specification and check before starting
- inspect your own work before handover
- record inspections, tests and commissioning data as required
- report nonconformities rather than concealing them

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Check you are working to the current drawing/specification/instruction
- carry out and record the required inspections and tests
- report defects and nonconformities as soon as they're found
- leave accurate handover/commissioning records

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Deviate from the specification without approval
- conceal or ignore a defect to save time
- sign off work that hasn't actually been checked

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Take photos of completed work before it's covered up or closed in
- double-check critical dimensions/settings before moving on
- raise queries early rather than guessing

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop and report immediately if a quality issue creates a safety risk
- quarantine nonconforming materials or equipment
- notify your supervisor before continuing

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Defect Reporting on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
