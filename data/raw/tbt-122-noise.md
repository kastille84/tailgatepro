# TBT-122 Noise

## Why It Matters
Noise matters because dust, noise and vibration exposure often causes harm gradually, so the controls have to be used every time, not just when it feels necessary.

## KEY HAZARDS
- Inhalation of harmful dust (e.g. silica)
- noise-induced hearing loss
- hand-arm vibration syndrome
- cumulative long-term health effects

## REQUIRED CONTROLS
- Use on-tool extraction or water suppression to control dust
- wear the RPE/hearing protection specified for the task
- limit exposure time on high-vibration tools and take breaks
- use low-vibration, well-maintained tools

## EMPLOYEES MUST
- Use dust extraction/suppression and RPE where specified
- wear hearing protection in designated noise zones
- monitor and limit trigger time on vibrating tools
- report symptoms such as tingling, numbness or hearing changes

## EMPLOYEES MUST NOT
- Carry out dust-generating work without extraction or suppression
- remove hearing protection in a marked noise zone
- exceed daily exposure limits on vibrating equipment

## GOOD PRACTICE
- Rotate tasks to reduce individual exposure time
- keep RPE clean and correctly fitted (face-fit tested where required)
- report tools that vibrate excessively for maintenance

## EMERGENCY / INCIDENT RESPONSE
- Stop the task if RPE or extraction fails
- move to fresh air if you experience breathing difficulty
- report exposure incidents so health surveillance can be arranged

## Supervisor Discussion
- What are the main hazards of Noise on our jobs, and which of today's controls matters most on this site?
- Which of these controls is easiest to skip when we're under time pressure — and what's the cost if it goes wrong?
- What would make you stop the job and ask for help?
